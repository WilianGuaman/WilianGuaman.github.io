Research

Power-system planning, operation and energy transition

My work combines optimization, power-system modeling, data analysis and applied engineering. The main line is long-term planning of hydro-dependent systems.

Doctoral dissertation

Long-Term Planning of Hydro-Dependent Power Systems

My dissertation asks how a power system that relies strongly on hydropower should plan long-lived investments when water availability, reservoir operation, transmission constraints and energy-policy pathways interact.

PARAMO is the modeling framework developed for this work. It couples annual investment decisions with monthly operation and evaluates generation, grid reinforcement, reconductoring, storage behavior, reserve, emissions, cost and adequacy under hydrological variability.

Hydrology and uncertainty

Water availability at the planning time scale

The hydrological component represents monthly availability sequences rather than a single annual average. This makes it possible to test when dry conditions occur, how long they persist and how reservoir behavior affects cost, network flows and supply adequacy.

Transmission planning

Expansion, reconductoring and geography

Several studies address alternatives to conventional transmission expansion. My work has examined reconductoring, repowering and reconfiguration, including the effect of Andean geography on transmission investment decisions.
