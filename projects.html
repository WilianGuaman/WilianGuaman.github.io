Doctoral research framework

PARAMO

PARAMO is the optimization framework developed in my doctoral research for long-term planning of hydro-dependent power systems. It couples annual investment decisions with monthly operation and represents generation expansion, transmission reinforcement, reconductoring, reservoir operation, reserves, hydrological uncertainty, unserved energy and policy pathways.

Interactive results and public data

PARAMO Ecuador Results Explorer

The public explorer provides direct access to scenario results from PARAMO studies. It currently includes the Ecuador 24-bus national planning case and a 6-bus demonstrator, with views for generation expansion, transmission and reconductoring, hydrology and reservoirs, system operation, emissions, reliability, costs, uncertainty and scenario comparison.

Teaching platform

POSEP

POSEP is a teaching platform that I use in courses related to power-system planning and operation. It brings together theory, datasets, optimization exercises, AMPL models and AI-supported workflows so that students can move from the mathematical formulation of a problem to solver results and their engineering interpretation.

ESPOCH-ONE

IEEE Humanitarian Technologies awarded USD 9,998 to ESPOCH-ONE. The project implemented an automatic chlorination system for the drinking-water supply in Licto. Work included electrical infrastructure, grounding, dosing-system integration, commissioning, operator training and formal handover to the local water board.

Angamarca community engineering project

The project in Angamarca combined a grain dryer, a grain mill and STEM activities with children and adolescents. I participated as professional coordinator of the SIGHT component, supporting the connection between engineering implementation and the work carried out with the local community.
